**SLIDE 2 -- FCC-ee and the CLD detector**
SPEAKER: Vincenzo Del Piano

WHAT TO SAY:
"FCC-ee is a proposed 100-kilometre electron-positron collider, studied here
at four centre-of-mass energies: 91 GeV at the Z pole, 160 at the WW
threshold, 240 at ZH production, and 365 at the top-pair threshold. The CLD
detector -- shown here from the original collaboration deck -- gives us the
silicon tracking, calorimetry and 2-tesla field we need to reconstruct jets,
leptons and missing energy precisely enough to do the physics in this talk.
At each energy point we're handed several anonymised Monte Carlo samples --
we don't know in advance what they are. Figuring that out is the first job."

---

**SLIDE 3 -- Method: four steps**
SPEAKER: Vincenzo Del Piano

WHAT TO SAY:
"Our logic through this whole talk is four steps: define what the Standard
Model predicts at a given energy, look for evidence that doesn't match that
prediction, interpret that evidence with a specific hypothesis, and then
validate -- or reject -- that hypothesis against the alternative. We'll run
through this twice today: once at 91 GeV, once at 365."

**SLIDE 4 -- Task A: identifying the samples (160 GeV)**
SPEAKER: Kobe Degeetere

WHAT TO SAY:
"At 160 GeV we're handed four anonymous samples. Task A is matching each one
to a known process using cuts on jet and lepton multiplicity, b-tagging and
missing energy, then checking whether the resulting invariant mass lands
where we'd expect. Two of the four are confidently identified this way. This
table shows the cuts and the cumulative efficiency -- every number here is
marked with an asterisk because the original talk only gives us the cut
definitions, not the absolute yields, so these are our own toy-calibrated
assumptions, not measured numbers."

---

**SLIDE 5 -- Higgs and WW mass peaks**
SPEAKER: Kobe Degeetere

WHAT TO SAY:
"For the Higgs candidate, two b-tagged jets with no leptons: the dijet mass
peaks right at 125 GeV. For the WW candidate, one lepton plus missing
energy plus two jets: the effective mass comes out close to the full
collision energy, 160 GeV, exactly what you'd expect from a semileptonic W
pair decay. In both cases our toy prediction tracks the reported points well
at the peak -- that's the actual identification evidence, not the cutflow
numbers."

**SLIDE 6 -- All five samples identified (365 GeV)**
SPEAKER: Saurav Bania

WHAT TO SAY:
"365 GeV is the most complete working point in the original material -- all
five samples get an assignment: top-pair production, electron-positron
annihilation to a fermion pair, ZZ, ZH, and WW. With up to four jets
available we can use b-tagging much more aggressively than at 160 to
separate these final states. Same caveat as before: every efficiency in
this table is a toy assumption, marked accordingly."

---

**SLIDE 7 -- Strongest evidence: ttbar and ZZ**
SPEAKER: Saurav Bania

WHAT TO SAY:
"These two give the cleanest evidence. For top-pair production, the
reconstructed mass sits at the top-quark scale, and it's the best
toy-to-data agreement of anything in this study -- chi-square over degrees
of freedom of 0.81. For the ZZ candidate, the dijet mass peaks cleanly at
the Z mass, 91 GeV, exactly as expected for one Z decaying hadronically."

---

**TRANSITION -- Andrea Maria**
"From SM identification to the excess -- once the samples are identified,
can the SM explain the observed structure?"

---

**SLIDE 8 -- An excess, not an SM sample (91 GeV)**
SPEAKER: Andrea Maria

WHAT TO SAY:
"Now a different question: is there something in the 91 GeV data that none
of the identified SM processes explains? With a tight selection -- missing
energy above 3 GeV, at least one lepton, a small impact parameter -- we see
a lepton-plus-jet excess. The proposed interpretation is a long-lived Heavy
Neutral Lepton at about 40 GeV. To test that, we compare the resonance
hypothesis against a flat, non-resonant background shape: the resonance
fits meaningfully better, chi-square over ndof 4.47 versus 5.81 for the flat
alternative."

---

**SLIDE 9 -- Independent cross-check**
SPEAKER: Andrea Maria

WHAT TO SAY:
"To make sure this isn't an artefact of one variable, we check a second,
independent observable -- the missing transverse momentum itself -- built
from the same hypothesis. It agrees well with the reported shape, chi-square
over ndof of 1.14. That's a genuinely separate check, not the same test
twice."

---

**SLIDE 10 -- Significance: which definition?**
SPEAKER: Andrea Maria

WHAT TO SAY:
"The original material quotes three different significance numbers for this
excess -- 5.79, 7.0 and 7.8 sigma -- without saying which is which. We
recomputed a background-free counting significance and got 7.9 sigma,
which lines up with the quoted 7.8. We can't reconstruct where the other two
numbers come from without more information, so we don't guess. And to be
clear: we are not applying any look-elsewhere correction here -- that needs
the actual number of independent search trials from the real analysis,
which we don't have, so we simply don't invent one."

**TRANSITION -- Jurjan Bootsma**
"The excess at 365 GeV -- testing reconstruction and background
hypotheses."

---

**SLIDE 11 -- The excess and the original method (365 GeV)**
SPEAKER: Jurjan Bootsma

WHAT TO SAY:
"At 365 GeV, a similar excess appears under a tighter selection -- low
missing energy, at least two jets and two leptons, a Z-veto, a b-veto. The
original material's method is the hadronic-W channel: only one jet is
assumed reconstructed on the production side, and the W mass -- and from it
the HNL mass -- is built from a fixed pairing of the second and third jet.
Importantly, the source material itself already flags this as a limitation:
it says sub-optimal pairing might lead to a large spread, and that other
candidates are possible. That caveat is about the original method, not a
description of an already-improved one."

---

**SLIDE 12 -- A kinematic-pairing test [CURRENT ANALYSIS]**
SPEAKER: Jurjan Bootsma

WHAT TO SAY:
"With four jets in the event there are three possible ways to pair two of
them into a W candidate. So we built a direct test, clearly marked as our
own current-analysis addition, not part of the original method: does always
using the fixed j2-j3 pairing versus picking whichever pairing sits closest
to the real W mass make a difference? The fixed pairing is only correct a
third of the time by construction, and it reproduces the broad, weak shape
you see in the original plots. Choosing the pairing closest to 80.4 GeV
instead gets the correct pairing 88% of the time, and both the W peak and
the HNL peak narrow substantially. That's quantitative evidence that the
original fixed pairing can mis-associate jets -- it supports improving the
reconstruction, it does not by itself prove there's an HNL there."

---

**SLIDE 13 -- Resonance vs. SM-tail, and significance**
SPEAKER: Jurjan Bootsma

WHAT TO SAY:
"We also tested whether this excess could just be a mis-measured tail of
the ZZ or WW processes we already identified at this energy. It doesn't fit
well -- chi-square over ndof of over 200, against about 4 for the resonance
hypothesis -- so we can disfavour that specific background explanation. The
quoted significance is 6.46 sigma. One caveat: we don't have a validated
background estimate behind that number from the source material, so we
don't present it as more settled than it is."

**SLIDE 14 -- Open questions**
SPEAKER: Greta Brianti

WHAT TO SAY:
"Before we conclude, the honest list of what's still unresolved. Most
importantly: we have two reconstructed HNL masses, about 40 GeV at 91 GeV
and about 150 GeV at 365. A single particle has one mass -- we are not
explaining this away, it's an open consistency question. The 365 GeV
background normalisation isn't established from the source. No
look-elsewhere trial count is available, so none is applied. Every cutflow
efficiency you saw is a toy assumption. And throughout, rejecting one
specific background hypothesis is not the same as proving a new particle."

---

**SLIDE 15 -- Chi-square per SM process**
SPEAKER: Greta Brianti

WHAT TO SAY:
"A compact summary of the SM validation. Only the processes where we
actually computed a toy-versus-data comparison get a number -- top pair and
ZZ at 365, Higgs and WW at 160. For the other three 365 GeV processes we
did not run that comparison, so they're marked not available rather than
guessed at."

---

**SLIDE 16 -- Flat vs. resonant hypothesis**
SPEAKER: Greta Brianti

WHAT TO SAY:
"And the core validation table for the two excesses side by side. At both
energies the resonance hypothesis fits better than the specific flat or
SM-tail alternative we tested. That's support for the resonance
interpretation, not proof of it. The last row is the jet mis-association
check from Jurjan's section: the fixed pairing is right a third of the
time, a W-mass-constrained pairing gets it right most of the time."

**SLIDE 17 -- Where this leaves us**
SPEAKER: Greta Brianti

WHAT TO SAY:
"So: five processes identified at 365 GeV, two at 160, each with numbers we
can trace back to either the source material or our own code -- never
invented. Two excesses, each consistent with a resonance hypothesis under
the specific tests we ran, each with real open caveats. This is exactly the
kind of small, carefully-validated signal FCC-ee's precision is meant to
let us take seriously -- provided every claim stays traceable. So, before we
close: what do you think is really in there?"
